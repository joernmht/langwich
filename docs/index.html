1The analogue case

Why paper, in an age of bottomless feeds?

Every language app shares a glass rectangle with your inbox, your group chats and a dozen infinite feeds — each engineered to interrupt you. langwich makes the opposite trade: the digital world generates, the analogue world teaches.

  • Paper doesn’t ping

    A worksheet has no notification badge, no autoplay, no “just one more” loop. The only thing that happens next is the word you write.

  • Handwriting sticks

    Writing by hand recruits motor and visual systems that tapping glass never touches — and study after study links it to better recall and deeper understanding.†

  • A worksheet ends

    Feeds are engineered to be bottomless. A sheet of paper is finished when it’s full — completion, not a streak, is the reward.

  • Easy on eyes and sleep

    Calm black-and-white pages made for print and e-paper: readable in full sunlight, and nothing glowing at your face at 11 pm.

  • Nothing to lose

    No account, no subscription, no streak to protect. Your worksheets are PDF files on your own disk — they’ll still open in twenty years.

  • Screens where they shine

    Let the AI do what it’s good at — telling a story, choosing the words, writing the tasks in seconds. Then take the paper somewhere the Wi-Fi can’t follow.

† For instance Mueller & Oppenheimer, Psychological Science (2014), on longhand note-taking and conceptual learning; van der Weel & van der Meer, Frontiers in Psychology (2024), on brain connectivity during handwriting.

2The workflow

From an idea to a printout in three steps

You talk, the AI writes, langwich checks the result and lays it out for paper. Nothing to install, no account with us, nothing to configure.

langwich is deliberately split in two: an AI writes every word, a small Python tool checks it and sets it on paper. langwich itself calls no API and needs no key and no account — and its renderer fetches nothing from the network: the PDF is made from the JSON, the bundled fonts and the picture the JSON names.

Say what you’d like

Your language, the one you are learning, your level — and what the story should be about: bread, night trains, your next holiday. Or send a photo, and the story is built around it.

The AI writes the story

A short story in scenes, with real facts and characters you want to follow. Around it: word lists, grammar tips and tasks that walk you through it, each with its answer. Want more? Ask for episode 2.

You get a calm PDF

langwich checks the AI’s work and sends mistakes back for fixing, so you don’t have to spot them. Then it lays everything out in lesson order: black and white, room to write, the answers at the back.

Ask for a prompt

langwich prompt --topic bread prints a complete brief for the AI: your languages and level, how to tell a story that carries true facts, the task kinds and the JSON format. Have a photo? --image photo.jpg copies it to data/pictures/ and makes it the starting point of the story.

Let any AI write the story

Paste the brief into Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or a model on your own computer. It answers with one JSON file: a story in scenes, true facts, vocabulary, grammar notes and every task with its answers. With a photo attached, it places numbered labels on it; without one, it can draw simple line art.

langwich checks and typesets

langwich validate catches slips — it even reads a chatty reply with a code fence around the JSON — and writes a repair prompt for the AI; with --quality it also holds the sheet to a design rubric. langwich render puts the tasks in lesson order and typesets the PDF — A4 or e-paper, with the answers at the back. The format is documented in the README.

3What lands on the page

A lesson in every story

Every worksheet follows the same arc through 8 stages: warm up before you read, then scene by scene — read a little, check what happened, look at the picture, work with the language, read on — then write something of your own and take it further. The AI picks from 13 kinds of task; langwich puts each one right after the scene it belongs to. (These numbers aren’t hand-written, by the way — they’re read from the code on every deploy.)

Before you read

warm_up

Meet the key words and make a guess about the story before its first line.

matchMatching
Pair each word with its meaning; a few spare answers keep it honest.

Scene by scene: what happened

gistdetail

After each scene: first the main events, then a closer look at facts, reasons and feelings.

true_falseTrue or false
Tick true or false, and correct the false statements.
multiple_choiceMultiple choice
Tick the one right answer.
order_eventsPut in order
Number the events in the order they happened.
questionsQuestions
Answer in writing, on lines sized for handwriting.

Scene by scene: the picture

picture

One scene has a picture — your photo, or simple line art the AI drew. Its tasks come right after you have understood the scene.

labelName it
Name the numbered objects in the scene picture; the word box gives the words, you add the article.
drawDraw it
No picture? Draw the scene yourself and label it.

Scene by scene: the language

formpractice

Notice the grammar the scene uses, then put the new words and forms into new sentences that carry the story on. A grammar box sits beside the task that practises it.

clozeFill the gaps
Fill the gaps — with a word box, first letters, base forms, translations or no help at all.
transformRewrite it
Rewrite sentences following a cue: another tense, another person.
word_buildingBuild the word
Put the parts together into one word: der Kaffee + die Kirsche.
dialogueDialogue
Fill in, or write, the missing lines of a conversation — with a few wrong words in the box.

Your turn — and take it further

productionepilogue

Write about the story or about yourself, then go looking for more in the language you are learning.

writingWrite
Write a text: a prompt, a first line, words to use, a word count — and a model answer at the back.
media_searchLook it up
Search online in the target language: search terms instead of links, and what to find out.

These are the usual places — any kind of task can sit at any stage. Around the tasks: a cover with the characters and your route through the sheet, glosses in the margin for words no task tests, “Did you know?” boxes with true facts, grammar boxes beside the task that practises them. At the back: a word list, the answers and a translation of every scene.

The same sheet on an e-reader

Every worksheet also comes in an e-reader size: one column, larger type, notes in boxes below the text. Ask for it when you make yours — or for a version with one task per page, so there is room for notes.

With --page epaper every worksheet is set at 157.8 × 210.4 mm in a single column; notes from the margin move into boxes below the text. Add --one-task-per-page for room to annotate, and --solutions separate to keep the answers in a second file.

5Make your own

Your own worksheet, in a chat with Claude

No code and no terminal. You add one file to Claude once; after that you just ask for worksheets in your own words, and Claude hands you the finished PDF.

  1. Download the langwich skill

    One small file that teaches Claude how to make langwich worksheets.

    Download langwich-skill.zip

  2. Add it to Claude — once

    On claude.ai or in the Claude desktop app (a free account is enough):

    • In Settings → Capabilities, check that Code execution and file creation is on (in newer versions: Create and edit files).
    • Go to Customize → Skills, click +, then Create skill → Upload a skill, and choose the file you downloaded. Leave the zip as it is.

  3. Ask for a worksheet

    Start a new chat and write what you want, for example:

    “Make me a worksheet for learning Spanish, level A2, about the markets of Valencia.”

    Claude asks two or three quick questions, suggests story ideas, writes the story, checks it and gives you a PDF to download. That takes a few minutes.

  4. Print it, or put it on your e-reader

    Claude also gives you a small .json file. Keep it: attach it next time and ask for “episode 2”, and the story goes on with the same characters.

Good to know

  • A photo can start the story. Attach one and write “build the story around this photo”. Claude numbers things in it, and you name them on paper.
  • Not sure of your level? Just describe it — “I can order in a café but not follow the news” — and Claude picks one.
  • Using Claude at work? Your administrator may have to allow skills and code execution first. If Claude can only make a web page instead of a PDF, open it and choose Print → Save as PDF.
  • ChatGPT, Gemini and others write good stories too, but they cannot run langwich in their chat, so they cannot hand you the finished PDF. Someone technical can set langwich up on your computer in about ten minutes — switch the page to to see how.
  • Mistakes? langwich catches mistakes in how the worksheet is put together, and Claude checks the language and the facts, but an AI can still be wrong now and then. If something looks odd, ask Claude in the same chat.

5Bring your own AI

Works with the AI you already use

langwich calls no API and stores no key. The writing comes from whichever assistant you already use, pay for, or run on your own computer. Four ways in:

The repository ships a /langwich slash command. Claude asks for your languages and level once, suggests a few story ideas, writes the JSON, validates and repairs it, looks over the language and the facts, and renders the PDF — all in one conversation. This is the most comfortable way in.

git clone https://github.com/joernmht/langwich
cd langwich
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -e .
claude
> /langwich

Add a topic, a photo path or “next episode” after /langwich. Claude uses the langwich in .venv (and installs it there if it is missing). Your defaults are kept in .langwich/profile.json. Works in the Claude Code CLI, the desktop app and on claude.ai/code.

No terminal at all? langwich-skill.zip is the same companion as an Agent Skill for claude.ai and the Claude desktop app: it bundles langwich, installs it in Claude’s code sandbox and hands back the PDF and the JSON (built by scripts/build_skill.py from skill/SKILL.md).

Any chat assistant that follows long instructions can write a worksheet — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude.ai, Le Chat. langwich prints the brief; you carry it over and bring the JSON back.

  1. Print the brief with your languages, level and topic.
  2. Paste prompt.txt into a new chat, or attach the file. Starting from a photo? Add --image — langwich copies the photo to data/pictures/ — attach the photo as well, and use a model that can see images.
  3. Save the reply as data/tides_en_de.json. Just the JSON object is best; if the model wrapped it in a code fence or chatted around it, langwich reads the object anyway and warns.
  4. Check it. If langwich finds problems, paste the repair prompt into the same chat and save the new reply over the file.
  5. Typeset it.
# 1. the brief (with a photo: add --image harbour.jpg and attach harbour.jpg too;
#    save the JSON in data/, where the copied photo is)
langwich prompt --source en --target de --level B1 --topic tides -o prompt.txt

# 4. check the JSON the AI wrote; if there are problems, make a repair prompt
langwich validate data/tides_en_de.json
langwich validate data/tides_en_de.json --prompt > repair.txt

# 5. typeset it → data/tides_en_de.pdf
langwich render data/tides_en_de.json

Add --save-profile once, and langwich remembers your languages, level, colour choice and device (never the story frame): next time langwich prompt --topic volcanoes is enough.

No cloud at all: langwich works just as well with a model on your own computer — in Ollama, LM Studio or llama.cpp. Small models do better with the shorter brief, and langwich’s repair loop catches what they get wrong.

# once: a copy of the model with a 32k context (Ollama's default is far too small)
printf 'FROM gemma3:12b\nPARAMETER num_ctx 32768\n' > Modelfile
ollama create gemma3-32k -f Modelfile

# a brief half as long, for models of 7–14B parameters
langwich prompt --compact --target es --level A2 --topic "a night market" -o prompt.txt

# paste prompt.txt into the model's chat, save its answer, then check it
langwich validate data/night_market_en_es.json

# problems? paste this repair prompt into the same chat, save the reply over the file
langwich validate data/night_market_en_es.json --prompt

langwich render data/night_market_en_es.json
  • Context: the compact brief is about 3,500 tokens and a finished worksheet another 6,000–12,000, so give the model at least 16k tokens of context — 32k for the repair loop, which sends the whole JSON back. A cut-off answer is not valid JSON. In Ollama, use the 32k copy made above (or start the server with OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH=32768 ollama serve); in LM Studio, set the context length when you load the model.
  • Bigger is better: larger models tell better stories and can take the full brief — leave out --compact.
  • Photos need a vision model, such as Gemma 3 or Qwen2.5-VL.

A fresh worksheet on your desk every morning. Claude Code’s print mode runs the whole loop unattended — it only needs permission to run langwich and to write into data/:

#!/bin/sh
# ~/langwich/daily.sh: one new worksheet a day, saved as data/<date>.pdf
cd "$HOME/langwich" || exit 1
PATH="$HOME/langwich/.venv/bin:$PATH"   # the langwich installed in .venv
day=$(date +%F)
langwich prompt --target de --level B1 -o "data/$day-prompt.md"
task="Read data/$day-prompt.md and write the worksheet it asks for to data/$day.json.
Choose a topic that no other worksheet in data/ has used yet.
Then run 'langwich validate data/$day.json' and fix what it reports until there are no errors.
Finally run 'langwich render data/$day.json'."
claude -p "$task" --allowedTools "Bash(langwich:*)" "Edit(data/**)"

Make it executable (chmod +x ~/langwich/daily.sh) and add two lines with crontab -e. Cron starts with a bare PATH, so name the folder that holds claude (command -v claude shows it); the script adds langwich’s .venv itself:

PATH=/usr/local/bin:/usr/bin:/bin:/home/you/.local/bin
30 6 * * * $HOME/langwich/daily.sh >> $HOME/langwich/data/cron.log 2>&1

The permissions are deliberately narrow: Bash(langwich:*) lets Claude run langwich and nothing else, Edit(data/**) lets it write only inside data/; reading files needs no permission. Log in to Claude Code once as the user the cron job runs as.

Rather stay offline? The same loop with a local model through Ollama — up to three repair rounds, then render:

#!/bin/sh
# ~/langwich/local.sh: the same with a local model (Ollama), fully offline
cd "$HOME/langwich" || exit 1
PATH="$HOME/langwich/.venv/bin:$PATH"
model=gemma3-32k   # gemma3:12b with a 32k context, made once:
ollama show "$model" >/dev/null 2>&1 || {
  printf 'FROM gemma3:12b\nPARAMETER num_ctx 32768\n' > data/Modelfile
  ollama create "$model" -f data/Modelfile
}
day=$(date +%F)
langwich prompt --compact --target de --level B1 -o "data/$day-prompt.md"
ollama run "$model" --format json < "data/$day-prompt.md" > "data/$day.json"
for round in 1 2 3; do
  langwich validate "data/$day.json" && break
  langwich validate "data/$day.json" --prompt > "data/$day-repair.md"
  [ -s "data/$day-repair.md" ] || break   # nothing the model can repair
  ollama run "$model" --format json < "data/$day-repair.md" > "data/$day-fixed.json" || break
  mv "data/$day-fixed.json" "data/$day.json"
done
langwich render "data/$day.json"

Any model you have pulled works in place of gemma3:12b — keep the 32k context (PARAMETER num_ctx 32768): the repair round sends the whole worksheet back. ollama run … --format json keeps the answer to pure JSON, and the loop stops when there is nothing the model can repair. Pair either script with an e-paper tablet or a duplex printer, and the day starts with a worksheet instead of a feed.

6Get started

A few commands to your first worksheet

You need Python 3.11 or newer; langwich goes into a virtual environment, .venv (in a new terminal, activate it again, or call .venv/bin/langwich). PDFs are made with WeasyPrint, which needs the Pango library from your system (brew install pango on a Mac; the README has the steps for Linux and Windows). The repository includes 10 complete worksheets, so you can print one before involving any AI at all — black-and-white duplex is fine.

Instructions are written in your language by the AI; langwich’s own page labels are built in for 6 languages (English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese), and for any other the AI writes those too. Then put your phone in another room.
